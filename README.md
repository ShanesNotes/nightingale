# Nightingale

Nightingale is building a causal clinical world: a native Rust physiology engine, research into learned patient dynamics, and an agent-native clinical workspace. The work begins with one patient. It is for simulation and teaching, not the care of a real person. [Engine requirements](docs/srs.md) · [Clinical workspace prototype](clinical-workspace/) · [Generative physiology research](.scratch/generative-physiology/map.md)

**It started with a question: how is AI going to be deployed in healthcare?** Clinical documentation systems were built as domain-specific charts for humans to append care into a history. Alongside that clinical purpose came the regulatory, billing and institutional requirements the documentation had to satisfy. These systems hold an enormous amount of knowledge, but they were not designed around humans and agents working together.

We have watched the emphasis move from prompt engineering to orchestrator swarms, context engineering, harness engineering and now workspace engineering. These are overlapping waves, but they keep returning to the same question: how can we, as humans, bolt agents onto our current systems? When attempting to engineer an agent-native EHR, we risk losing two important things: human visibility into the data, and an inspectable account of what evidence the agent used, why it mattered and when. An explanation generated after the fact is not enough. We need traceable evidence, uncertainty and actions that the clinician can examine.

## The nuance in the margins of medicine

Documentation, waveforms, interventions and outcomes all matter. But even together they leave gaps: the bedside observation that was never charted, the treatment given before a measurement, the reserve behind a seemingly normal vital sign, and the effort required to keep that number normal. A mean arterial pressure of 65 maintained without support does not describe the same clinical state as 65 maintained by escalating vasopressors.

Consider norepinephrine. Patients who receive it are often critically ill, and some die despite treatment. A model can learn an association between the drug and death, then mistake that association for the drug's effect. This is **confounding by indication**: the reason a treatment was given is itself related to the outcome. Illness severity can drive both treatment and mortality. The danger is learning that norepinephrine “kills” patients from records of clinicians trying to save them. That is an illustration of a causal error, not a claim about what a particular model has learned, nor a claim that the drug cannot cause harm. [Critical-care research on confounding](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4432515/) · [A norepinephrine study discussing this limitation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9547317/)

Timing makes this harder. A clinician changes treatment in response to a deteriorating patient; treatment changes the physiology that informs the next decision. Which observations are collected, and when, also reflects clinical concern. A model can learn the pattern of care without learning the effect of care. The question becomes counterfactual: what might have happened to this same patient, from this same state, under a different intervention? The chart contains the path taken. It does not contain every path that could have been taken.

## Start with the patient

For Nightingale, we are taking a zoomed-out approach to this problem. We want AI to elevate the quality of care given to the patient, not simply offload tedious work while leaving the foundations unchanged. That has brought us to the first-principles engineering of a physiology simulation engine in native Rust. Huge credit goes to the [Pulse Physiology Engine team](https://pulse.kitware.com/) for making their work open source. It gave us a foundation to learn from and a reference to question, rather than a ceiling on what we could build.

The ambition is the highest physiological fidelity we can substantiate: first, a healthy patient who reliably lives through ordinary activity; then pathophysiology and pharmacology acting on that same body. The [first milestone](docs/adr/0004-first-milestone.md) is a reference adult living through 48 simulated hours of sleep, meals and posture changes, with conserved amounts, appropriate readings and a return toward their own morning baseline. The monitor measures the body. The chart holds what was documented. Neither gets to overwrite the patient to make a case follow its script. That foundation matters when AI agents are doing the engineering. An incomplete slice can become the next agent's assumed foundation, and shortcuts compound. We want bounded work that closes real mechanisms and contracts, with checks that expose what is missing, rather than a convincing demonstration held together by physiology that has not been built. Numerical correctness, physiological validity and educational usefulness each need their own evidence.

## From physiology to a learned clinical world

If we can do this rigorously, the engine's outputs can help curate high-quality synthetic datasets: patient variation, underlying state, observations, interventions and responses over time. Branching from the same checkpoint could let us compare alternative courses of care within the model. Those datasets could supplement open clinical data and help explore the causal gaps left by documentation alone.

Synthetic data does not remove confounding or establish human validity by itself. The generator can be wrong, its intervention policy can introduce bias, and a learned model can reproduce those mistakes with impressive precision. We need independent evidence, explicit limits and experiments that distinguish copying the teacher from learning a response that holds beyond the training conditions. The [generative physiology research](.scratch/generative-physiology/issues/01-concept-brief.md) begins with that distinction.

Enter the longer-term ambition: a **generative clinical world model**. A latent-state model, a diffusion model, a hybrid mechanistic-neural model, or another approach may prove useful. The architecture remains a research question. What matters is whether it can carry a persistent patient through time, respond to assessment and intervention, and represent different plausible trajectories without losing physiological consistency. Training on simulation is a route to investigate, not a substitute for validation.

## Coming full circle

This brings us back to an agent-native clinical workspace: a place to explore how humans and agents assess, document, communicate and reason around one patient. The human should be able to see the evidence, distinguish observation from interpretation, inspect the basis for an agent's work and retain clinical judgment. Institutional clinical practice guidelines belong in that work, with their sources and applicability visible. The goal is to push toward better care and better experiences of learning and practicing medicine. Nightingale explores that ambition in simulation; it does not authorize use in real patient care.

## Explore the work

The repository runs coupled synthetic bodies and includes a clinical workspace prototype that replays a recorded synthetic run rather than controlling the Rust engine live. It does not yet demonstrate a clinically validated reference patient or a completed generative clinical world model. Read the [requirements](docs/srs.md), [decision records](docs/adr/), [solver contract](docs/architecture/solver-contract.md), [13-system atlas](docs/atlas/), [glossary](GLOSSARY.md) and [checked statecharts](docs/statecharts/). The [body work map](.scratch/body/map.md) and [research work map](.scratch/generative-physiology/map.md) distinguish build work from research. This manifesto explains the ambition; the requirements and decision records govern the engine.

For engine checks, use Python 3.11 or newer, the Rust toolchain pinned in `rust-toolchain.toml`, and Node 24 or newer with npm for the clinical workspace gate. From a clone, run `.agents/skills/verify-nightingale/ensure-prereqs.sh`, then `cargo check --workspace --locked`, `cargo test --workspace --locked` and `tools/verify`. The setup script installs the pinned toolchain and enables the pre-push check when no hook is already configured. The [verification skill](.agents/skills/verify-nightingale/SKILL.md) documents the merge proof. There is no standalone engine application; tests exercise the synthetic bodies. The [clinical workspace verification guide](.agents/skills/verify-clinical-workspace/SKILL.md) covers that separate prototype.
