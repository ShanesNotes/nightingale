# Blood carries heat into the store a bed sits in, and each card reads a store's temperature through a grant

Body ticket 12's thermostat must carry heat to the skin at the skin bed's own flow, sense the skin's temperature at the skin thermoreceptor, narrow and widen the skin arteriole, and make sweat. Sweat touches four places. NS releases acetylcholine at the skin junction, the junction that supplies the skin bed. MT computes the sweat rate at the skin bed. The sweat's water and salts leave the skin bed's interstitium. Its heat leaves the skin heat site's store, which is the store the skin bed sits in and the one the skin thermoreceptor reads. On main none of these reads compiles (native-contract 102). The decision: the map puts the skin bed inside the skin heat site, so containment gives blood's carriage a heat route from the core store to the skin store. CV posts that carriage at the bed from a read of the bed's one arterial inflow. MT is the one owner of each tissue heat store's temperature, `T_0 + H / C` with that store's own capacity. CV and NS read a store's temperature, and MT reads the skin junction's acetylcholine effect, only through a grant: a typed object that reaches the field and the place the reader is entitled to and nothing else, so a wrong read is a type error or a compile refusal. The contract PR builds all of it dormant, each new interface tested in a synthetic assembly of its own, and PR 12b turns it on in the body.

Why this design. Two designs were made apart and agreed on the relation, the carriage, the temperatures, the skin arteriole's read and the sweat row. They differed on the reads. This one gives each reader a grant. The other gave every holder of a per-site port the same reads at any place, so a wrong place or ligand compiled. Its reason for a new kind of catalog entry, that two receivers of one field make two rows, is false: a coupling field's identity is its owner and local name (`Reads` in `crates/ng-contract/src/catalog.rs`), `FragmentBuilder::receive` declares no row, and the coupling test's nervous fragment already receives `CoreTemperature` (`crates/ng-contract/tests/coupling.rs`, `a_loop_of_two_couplings_is_one_block_of_coupled_residuals`).

## Considered options

- An explicit relation from each bed to the store it gives heat to, with containment left alone. Both designs rejected it. CV would carry heat once for each incoming vascular edge of a bed assigned to a non-core store, so an edge kernel would need a way to post to a store, and the relation would be a sealed anatomical declaration and a checked row outside the 38 arrows. It buys flexibility only for a heated bed with two feeds, and this map has none. Here it computes the same one paired column.
- One temperature field stated by MT's port, with the core as the containment root's entry, arrow 91's field renamed and the census at 17 wired, 32 held and 6 mixed. Rejected: it adds a census kind and a rename for the same rows, and it rests on the false premise above.
- Place reads generic over the owner and the site kind (the core's, a pool's, a store's) and a generic junction effect for MT. Rejected: any holder of a per-site port could read it at any place, and MT could read any ligand at any junction.
- A separate PR for the map's one changed parent. Rejected: a full proof for one line. Kept as PR 12b's first commit.
- A carriage coefficient per amount of water. Rejected: research 16's CV-082 is heat per volume per kelvin, so the contract converts the flow to volume.
- A temperature row as a solver block of its own, as a cost argument. Rejected: conduction reads the temperature and feeds heat back into the amount it reads.
- An amount read at a landmark, by fixing the landmark arm of `Source::Amount` in compile. Rejected: no caller needs it, and it would also let NS sample its own release route's amount at the pacemaker. NS reads the skin's temperature as a field at the landmark, which resolves at the landmark's site today (`at_landmark` in `crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs`).

## Consequences

The relation and the flow:

1. Blood's carriage at a bed runs from the core store to the nearest tissue heat store above the bed: from the bed's parent toward the root, the first tissue site with a thermal energy pool. A bed has a carriage route exactly when that store differs from the core store. Today the `HeatRoute::CarriedByBlood` arm of `reaches_of` in `crates/ng-contract/src/compile.rs` pairs the core store with the heat of the bed's parent only.
2. So a site with no heat pool between a bed and its store is passed through, two beds in one store get two routes, a bed whose nearest store is the core store gets none, and a store with no bed, such as the airway store, gets conduction and a temperature with no carriage.
3. Each bed's own heat still goes from its bed heat to the core store, as `HeatRoute::FromBeds` does today.
4. Compile finds each bed's store from the schema's declared pools and the containment tree. There is no second anatomical declaration, and nothing of it is kept in accepted state. A read of a placed store whose root or destination is missing refuses.
5. `bed_inflow` on a CV `CapillaryBed` kernel reads the signed flow of the bed's one incoming vascular edge. It matches the whole vascular network by destination, classifies the edge as `edge_flow` does, and binds the circulation edge's algebraic row or a valve's flow memory. It refuses zero or several feeders, a sinusoid the hepatic card owns, and an edge with no flow row. Today a flow read outside an edge context is `NoSuchRead`, and `inflow` is the urinary edge's (`crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs`).
6. The flow is signed in the network's direction, and the paired column debits and credits one signed extent. Any choice by direction belongs to CV's law.
7. The carriage law converts the water flow with `volume_flow`, the schema's partial-volume coefficient, so its coefficient is heat per volume per kelvin. Its rate is `c_blood × volume flow × (T_core − T_tissue)`, with the two temperatures read as arrow 90's two coordinates: the core-to-skin difference drives carriage.
8. `heat_carried` returns a `BloodCarriage`, whose one method `post` takes a CV `CapillaryBed` kernel of `Rate<Energy>` and posts it through `transfer_at`. It gives no amount reads. A request still owes its law when the caller drops the token. Today `heat_carried` in `crates/ng-contract/src/scope.rs` returns a raw `SiteRoute<'b, Cardiovascular, CapillaryBed, Energy>`, and its one user, `blood_carries_heat_from_the_core_store_to_the_store_a_bed_sits_in_at_that_bed` in `crates/ng-contract/tests/routes.rs`, moves to `post`. Other site routes keep their amount reads.

The temperatures:

9. MT defines each tissue heat store's temperature once, `T_0 + H / C`, in the kernel where the store's amount and capacity are. The core store's is arrow 91's scalar `core_temperature` at the body, declared through the existing `CoreTemperature` coupling. Each other store's is `tissue_temperature` at its site, declared by MT's own declaration and owing those rows alone. Every tissue heat store but the core gets its row whatever transfer laws a fragment asks for, since its sites come from the schema's pools. Today `Reaches::of` in `crates/ng-contract/src/compile.rs` builds routes only for the families the fragments request. MT's grant of the core's heat lands at the root, and its grant of a store's heat lands only on a thermal pool of a tissue site.
10. Each store has its own capacity: one positive body binding for the core store and one positive site-keyed binding for each other store.
11. `OxygenDebtPerBed` and `CoreTemperature` keep their fields and stay issuable apart. Today `emit` owes a definition for every field it issues (`FragmentBuilder::emit` in `crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs`), so widening the debt coupling would put a temperature on MT's debt emitter in `crates/ng-metabolism/src/burn.rs`.
12. One MT fragment that declares the core's temperature and the other stores' temperatures once each is legal. Declaring either one a second time in that fragment is `NamedTwice`.
13. MT declares the core scalar and the other stores' rows apart, and NS holds two handles on arrow 91. That leaves every row, field and pin of PR 12a where it is. It is looked at again when a third card needs a store's temperature.
14. Arrow 90's held `temperature`, held today by CV because "CV's laws run at a reference temperature" (`crates/ng-contract/src/catalog.rs`), becomes wired for ticket 12. CV reads it as two coordinates, the core and the tissue end of a bed's carriage route, and only in the context of a bed with a non-core route. `oxygen_debt_per_bed` stays wired for ticket 9.
15. Arrow 91 keeps `core_temperature`, its type, name and ticket, and gains `skin_temperature` for ticket 12: MT's field read by NS at the landmark `SkinThermoreceptor` in a body kernel, with no skin row of its own.
16. The landmark's sampled site must be a tissue heat store serving a skin bed. The landmark's height site is not where it reads.
17. Arrow 78 keeps `myocardial_work` wired for ticket 9 and `flow_per_bed` held by CV. The hold's reason becomes "MT's oxygen supply arrives by plasma transport. CV reads its own inflow for carriage." Only the wording moves.
18. Arrow 87 and every other arrow keep their entries. Shared substrate S10 gains one reader: MT reads RC's acetylcholine effect at the junction supplying the skin's beds. It sits beside the substrate readers, outside the 38 arrows.

The sweat and the skin arteriole:

## Limits

- One well-mixed heat store for the beds it holds, and one CV-owned feeder for each carrying bed. Two hydraulic feeds into one heated bed, such as portal and arterial delivery, refuse carriage until the model gives them a mixing rule or carriage per edge.
- A bed that exchanges heat with two stores needs a declared partition.
- More than one skin bed needs a sweat aggregate in place of the one-site row (`NotOneSite`).
- Separate dilator and sudomotor transmitters need separate receptor grants and a new evidence decision.

None of these is needed by the 13-system map or by PRs 12b to 12d.
