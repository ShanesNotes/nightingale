# The first milestone is a healthy man stable over two ordinary days

The first milestone is the ICRP 89 reference adult male living two ordinary days: asleep and supine at night, awake and seated by day, with three meals and drinks whose energy equals what he spends, in thermoneutral room air at sea level. He is healthy and stable when he passes three checks over those 48 simulated hours: the certificate shows that every amount balances and changes from one day to the next no more than a healthy man's does; a healthy-range check shows that each reading in the lens stays inside its healthy range for what he is doing at that moment; and a return check shows that 24 and 48 hours after admission, at the same time of day, he meets his anchors again by his own physiology. Each check sees a failure the others miss, and an ordinary day is the one a learner's patient lives.

## Considered options

- The certificate alone, as [pulse 0006](https://github.com/ShanesNotes/nightingale-pulse/blob/bdc01c567f6cadacbd96728c0600a2e052882a59/docs/decisions/0006-first-milestone-stable-healthy-body.md) had it: rejected. The certificate judges amounts only. It passes a body whose ledger balances while its blood pressure sits where no healthy man's does, and one whose set points and controller memories drift while every amount holds.
- The certificate and the healthy-range check, without the return check: rejected. A healthy range spans many healthy men, so this man could drift across it for the whole run and still pass. The return check holds him to his own anchors, and it needs no new sources, because the anchors already exist.
- A fixed list of vitals and labs in this record, as the healthy-range check's set: rejected. The lens can grow past the list, and a learner would then see readings that no check holds.
- Only the anchored quantities, as that set: rejected. The anchors were chosen to solve the start state, not to cover what a learner sees.
- One clinical normal range all day, from a textbook or a laboratory: rejected. It misjudges him asleep and after a meal.
- Within-subject variation around his anchors, as the healthy range: rejected. It measures him against himself, which is the return check's job, and his anchors exist only for 06:55.
- A narrower band, such as the middle half, because a reference man should look typical: rejected. Few studies report one, and it judges how typical he is, not whether he is healthy.
- A range covers only the activities, population and method its study measured: rejected. It would need a study for every combination, even to show that nothing changes, and would stall the milestone with no gain in rigor.
- A gap leaves the reading unchecked during that activity, with every gap listed: rejected. Checked would mean checked where a study happened to look, and listed gaps tend to stay.
- The closest activity's range fills a gap: rejected. It compares him with men doing something else.
- No inference in any healthy range: rejected. The claim would wait on a study nobody ran, even when two sources already give the answer.
- Inference may fill any healthy range, even one with no sourced range to adjust: rejected. The check could then rest entirely on the model's judgment.
- One condition held for all 48 hours, fed continuously at balance, lying still, with the clock held: rejected. It needs only one set of healthy ranges, but no man lives that way, and it hides the swings a healthy body has to show, such as blood pressure falling at night and glucose rising after a meal.
- Fasting: rejected. A healthy body without intake loses water and fuel, so stable would mean a rate of loss, not balance.
- The checks read what a learner sees, error and artifacts included: rejected. A healthy man near the edge of a range would fail on a noise draw, and the verdict would change with the observation seed, which changes nothing in the body (I9), and with how often the lens samples.
- The checks read the body's quantity before any method, such as the pressure in the aorta: rejected. A range measured by cuff would then judge a pressure no cuff measures.
- A return within 1.96 within-subject SDs, which treats admission as his set point rather than as one morning: rejected. A model whose mornings vary as much as a real man's would fail by chance on at least one of many anchors on most runs.
- A return to the same numbers, within the solver's tolerance: rejected. It tests the numerics, and it fails on the seeded variability the run has by design.
- Drift held to the numerical floor, as closure is: rejected. It catches the smallest leak, but it fails on the swings the run's seeded variability makes by design, and the floor is a model default, not a source.
- Drift held to a fixed share of each amount's daily turnover: rejected. It scales with each amount, but no study measured the share.
- Closure only for an amount no method can measure, its drift reported by name and held through the readings it drives: rejected. A runaway that no reading shows would pass unseen, and a model's pretraining can reason soundly from the sourced flows. The price is a pass bar set by a model's reasoning, so that reasoning starts from sourced flows, names its search, displays as inferred and counts in the verdict.
- No exception, so such an amount blocks the claim as any gap does: rejected. No source can ever cover it, so the milestone could pass only if the model stopped conserving it, which trades fidelity for a pass.
- The milestone as stage 1's done-check only, a line in the SRS's scope: rejected. A later stage could then break the healthy man without any test failing.
- The milestone in this record only: rejected. Records rank below the SRS, and tests come from its requirements and the anchors.

## Consequences

- Admission is at 06:55, awake and supine after the overnight fast, before rising or eating, which is how resting values are measured. The anchors hold at that moment, and the return check runs at each later 06:55.
- The day's numbers (sleep hours, meal times and shares, fluid volume, sodium, room conditions) are clinical values in the packet (record 0005). Each is sourced, or inferred where no source covers it, as a parameter is (record 0003).
- Whatever a learner can see on the healthy man is checked. The set grows with the lens, and a new lens applied to the milestone's replay reruns the check without changing physiology (I7). A quantity that no reading shows is held only by the certificate and the return check.
- A healthy range is the central 95% of healthy adults over the averaging window of a primary study with a locator, or else of a textbook value at the textbook tier with its edition and page, kept in the healthy-range set (record 0005). A study that reports only a mean and SD gives mean ± 1.96 SD, marked as derived. One range covers every activity in the day, both sexes, every adult age and every measurement method, unless a source shows that one of these shifts the reading; then the range must match him in it. Nothing finer than the day's activities is ever matched.
- The checks read each reading as its method gives it, at its site and over its window, with no random error drawn from the observation seed. The return check reads each anchor's method and window the same way. The healthy-range check tests every sliding window of its study's length, or each whole period when the study averaged over one, such as the night, against the range for the activity the window lies in, so a reading cannot leave its range between the moments a check looks. A window across a change takes the change's own range wherever a source shows the change shifts the reading, as when he sits up at 07:00 and his pressure dips for a few seconds.
- The return check allows each anchor the change a clinician would call real between two mornings: its reference change value without the analytical part, 1.96 × √2 × the within-subject variation of that reading by its method, from published biological variation studies of healthy adults. The variation lives in the healthy-range set, since it only judges the body (record 0005), under a healthy range's rules for sources, gaps and inference.
- The certificate holds closure to the numerical floor, a model default in validation, since an amount that fails to balance is an error in the arithmetic, never in the body. It holds drift to what a healthy man's amounts do on a fixed diet, as his is: each amount may change over any 24 hours by up to 1.96 times its within-subject variation, the SD of a healthy adult's own change in it over a day, from metabolic balance studies. That variation lives in the healthy-range set under a healthy range's rules for sources, gaps and inference (record 0005).
- An amount fails the certificate if it swings further than a healthy man's or keeps moving because nothing brings it back. A drift slower than his daily swing passes the 48 hours, and only a longer run would show it.
- An amount no method can measure in a living person, such as the whole-body catecholamine moiety or a held ligand surrogate, takes its within-subject variation from an inference record that reasons from the sourced flows into and out of it, such as the day-to-day variation of its 24-hour urinary excretion. The record states why no method can measure the amount and names the search that found none, it displays as inferred, the verdict counts it, and a source replaces it if a method appears. A reading's gap blocks the claim because a study could close it; no study can close this one, and the certificate is the only check on an amount no reading shows.
- An amount a study could measure but none has blocks the claim until a source covers it, as a reading does. Charge holds to the numerical floor, since physics holds the body's net charge at zero.
- The random error a learner sees needs its own check, against the device accuracy studies its numbers come from, outside the milestone.
- Where a source shows a shift but no study gives the range, an inference record adjusts a sourced range by the sourced shift: a sleep range for arterial CO2, for example, becomes one for end-tidal CO2 through a sourced gap between the two. The inference record names the search that found no study and displays as inferred, the verdict counts it, and the first source that covers the gap replaces it. Only a model writes one: a person sets rules like these, never a clinical number (rule 16).
- A reading with no sourced range at all blocks the milestone until a source covers it or the reading leaves the lens, since an inference would have nothing sourced to adjust. The monitor cannot leave the lens (SRS section 6). A block stops only the claim that the milestone passed, never building, the arena or any ticket.
- A healthy range is wide, so a reading near its edge, healthy but unusual, still passes. The anchors and the return check hold him at 06:55, and nothing holds his night or his afternoon to typical values.
- The reference man is a packet. Another adult is another packet, not other code.
- The milestone is SRS invariant I19, so it binds every later stage: work on disease, chance or interventions must leave a healthy adult with nothing done to them healthy and stable, and every reviewed packet of a healthy adult must pass it, not only the reference man's.
- The arena runs only synthetic fixtures, so there the three checks run on obviously synthetic targets, which tests the checks and not the body. Only a run of a reviewed packet can pass the milestone.
