## Answer

A number quoted in a row says what the source found, so ticket 05 knows where to look. `Holds for` cites a source where one states a range and says "model default" where none was found. "Model adaptation" means a sourced form was changed to fit the contract, and the row says how.

The reading depth follows each source. "Text" is the evidence store's extracted text at the pages named. "Page image" means the page was read from the rendered original, because its text layer is a scan's or drops the equation. "Open full text" is a work outside the store, read through Europe PMC or PubMed Central. "Abstract" is the abstract alone, from Europe PMC. No page cited here is one the store marks as OCR. Stolwijk 1971 has a scan's text layer, so its equations and tables were checked against page images. Gagge, Stolwijk and Nishi 1971 is not in the store. ### The interfaces with research 11, 13 and 14

- **Research 11.** The airway's water and heat are research 11's row, and they leave the core store, as Gagge's Eq 12 takes respiratory loss from the core. Research 11's tissue respiration row turns each bed's oxygen use into heat. The sleep gain and shivering act on that row's `D_bed`, so heat production by state needs no heat law of its own. Kingma's supplement, on hand for this ticket, gives respiratory heat at rest, evaporative and convective together, as a fixed 8% of the metabolic rate (Eq A2). Research 11 lists that supplement as not on hand for the dry heat of warming inspired air, so the form is there for research 11's owner.
- **Research 13.** The heat a meal adds is research 13's form. This ticket gives the checks above and stops there. A clock gain on the resting rate would also meet research 13's fuels, and rung 0 leaves it out (Decisions).
- **Research 14.** Sweat's water, sodium and chloride are a way out of the water and salt whose kidney and hormone forms research 14 gives. The sweat gland row sets how much leaves, and its concentration form says how sweat's salt follows its rate.

### Decisions

- **Gagge's two stores over Stolwijk's 25.** Body ticket 12 keeps two stores. Gagge's model has exactly two, Stolwijk is its second author, and Boron gives the same two paths from core to skin. Kingma's network has no heat capacities, so it cannot hold a store's change.
- **A constant radiative coefficient.** Gagge and Stolwijk hold `h_r` constant, and Stolwijk states the combined form within about 5% for 10 to 40 °C. A fourth power needs products of temperatures, and Kingma's cube needs the temperature-over-temperature row research 11 asked for. The table has neither. Kingma's factor `((T + 273.15) / 298)^3` moves from 0.95 at 20 °C to 1.05 at 30 °C, which is arithmetic on the form.
- **Diffusion follows the room.** Gagge's `w_d E_max` makes the skin's insensible loss follow humidity, air speed and clothing, which the boundary delivers. Stolwijk's fixed 9 kcal/h through skin (p. 15) leaves out the room's vapour, an effect he judges small.
- **A weighted sum, not a product.** Stolwijk's sweat derivation finds sweating linear in mean skin temperature at a fixed internal temperature (pp. 39 to 40 and 50 to 51). Wenger 1975 fits forearm flow during exercise as linear in core and skin temperature (J Appl Physiol 38:58, abstract), and Cheng takes the skin's share from the slope of the core threshold against mean skin. The product terms rest on Benzinger's shivering data (Stolwijk p. 58) and on Gagge's reading of Hardy and Stolwijk for sweating at rest (Gagge Eq 21). Tikuisis's fit rejects the product for shivering. Under a product, a cool room cannot start shivering until the core falls below its set point, which is arithmetic on the form.
- **One cholinergic command for sweat and dilation.** Levick's sudomotor cholinergic fibres cause skin vasodilation as well as sweating (p. 262), Sessler puts the active dilation threshold near the sweating threshold, and body ticket 01 gives the skin one junction. Stolwijk's two commands weigh core against skin as 11 and 15.6. Noll infers more vasodilator traffic and less sudomotor traffic in NREM sleep (abstract), so the two can part. A later rung that needs them apart adds a second junction or the partner transmitter.
- **Active dilation stays, carried by acetylcholine.** Levick puts heat dilation in non-acral skin mainly down to sympathetic cholinergic vasodilator activity, with VIP and nitric oxide as partners (p. 287), and Charkoudian credits it with 80 to 90% of skin dilation in whole-body heating (Mayo Clin Proc 78:603, abstract). Guyton gives heat dilation as the inhibition of sympathetic constrictor centres (p. 872), and Berne and Levy as decreased sympathetic activity (p. 237). VIP and nitric oxide are not species, so acetylcholine carries the dilator effect at rung 0. Atropine blocks the dilation only in part (Levick p. 262) or not at all (Boron pp. 591 to 592), so a rung with drugs needs the partner transmitter.
- **The clock moves the set point, not heat production.** Kingma 2012 cites a rhythm in heat production of +10% at noon and -6% at night against its mean (pp. 1978 to 1979, citing Kräuchi), and Eckert puts a quarter of the daily swing in production and three quarters in conductance (pp. 715 to 716). Rung 0 forces one driver, the set point, and the controller carries its swing to heat loss. A clock gain on the resting rate is left out. So are Tayefeh's dead band, twice as wide at 03:00 (Pflugers Arch 435:402, abstract), and a change in vasomotor slope by time of day, which Aoki reports (Am J Physiol 281:R591, abstract) and Stephenson's forearm data hint at. Stephenson notes the rhythm may be more complex than a shift of the reference.
- **Two harmonics, not one cosine.** Windred and Barrett both fit a 12 h harmonic beside the 24 h one, and Barrett recommends it over a single cosine. With the clock's cosine and sine, the 12 h terms cost only products.
- **Sleep steps the set point, not the gain.** Barrett finds core temperature lower after sleep onset, and Sagot finds the sweating threshold lower in slow-wave sleep than in stages 1 and 2, with the slope unchanged. No source on hand compares the gain awake and asleep in people. Eckert describes a lower set point and a lower gain in the slow-wave sleep of mammals (pp. 704 to 705). Rung 0 keeps the gain, a model default.
- **Total activity measures the sympathetic gain.** The slice's release is linear in traffic, so the gain takes the measure that counts both bursts and their size, Hornyak's total activity. Somers's 41% is burst amplitude alone, in stage 4, the deepest stage. One NREM gain weights the stages over a night when ticket 05 binds it.
- **One sleep gain for every bed.** Only the brain has a sourced fall by bed, 25% lower oxygen use in stages 3 and 4 (Madsen 1991, J Appl Physiol 70:2597, abstract). Body ticket 25 puts one gain on each bed's rate, and Guyton puts the fall down to muscle tone and the nervous system (p. 864).
- **Seated keeps the awake rate.** Levine's cost of sitting still is smaller than its scatter, and the factor of 1.3 likely includes movement that rung 0 lacks (body ticket 01, NS), which is an inference.

### What the contract lacks

No form needs an operator the kernel lacks. Each uses `add`, `subtract`, `multiply`, `divide`, `scale`, `exp`, `ln`, `smooth_max`, `smooth_min` and `deviation`, all on `Kernel` (`crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs`), and the set point's memory rate is a deviation times a speed, which the identity table holds. Water and its latent heat as one transfer need no new row either, because energy is a substance and the table already turns one substance's rate times a ratio of two substances into the other's rate. These gaps are new.

- **Heat has no identity rows.** A store's temperature needs heat over a heat capacity. Conduction and dry loss need a conductance times a temperature difference. Blood's heat needs a heat capacity per volume times a volume flow times a temperature difference. The table (`crates/ng-contract/src/quantity.rs`) has none of these rows, and research 11 listed only temperature over temperature, which the vapour pressure form also needs. Evaporation's product already has a row if its coefficient is declared as a volume flow, because a watt per pascal is a cubic metre per second (`Product<Pressure, Rate<Volume>>`). The body's surface and clothing's insulation have no dimension. So rung 0 holds the whole-body conductances, such as `K_min A` and `h A`, as parameters, and takes clothing as a count of clo.
- **No cosine.** The set point's target needs the cosine and sine of the clock's phase. `Phase` is a dimension that memory may hold, and `crates/ng-contract/src/catalog.rs` already types arrow 89's `circadian_phase` as one, but no operator takes its cosine or sine. Posture already arrives as a sine (`crates/ng-contract/src/boundary.rs`), so ticket 05 can deliver the clock as its cosine and sine.
- **No speed.** Air speed's square root needs speed over a reference speed. Ticket 05 can deliver air speed as its ratio to 0.1 m/s, or the table gains a speed.
- **A bed's heat stays at its bed.** `energy_into` sends a reaction's heat to the thermal pool at its own bed, and the thermal space holds one store on the body's site besides a bed's own (`crates/ng-contract/src/scope.rs`). Body ticket 01 keeps the core's heat on the body's site and the skin's on a skin heat site. So each bed's heat, the skin bed's included (heat stores row), needs a transfer to the core store. The smallest is first order in the bed's pool at a declared speed, so the pool holds no temperature of its own (model default). Stolwijk's form, where each compartment exchanges with central blood by its flow (p. 44), would make every bed a store of its own, which body ticket 12's non-goals rule out.
