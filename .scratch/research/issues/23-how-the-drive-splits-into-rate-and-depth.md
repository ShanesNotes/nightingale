## Answer

One primary study on hand gives the split under a rising chemical drive in man: Clark and von Euler 1972, seven healthy men rebreathing from a bell of oxygen. Up to a tidal volume of 1.5 to 2 times its resting value, inspiration's length stays fixed, so depth carries the whole rise and the rate stays at rest. Above that volume inspiration shortens as a hyperbola of tidal volume and expiration follows it, so depth and rate rise together. Depth has no ceiling in that range. The split below takes that form. It replaces the model default this ticket held before, which put a ceiling on depth at the textbooks' inflation-reflex volume and let rate carry everything above it. Each statement carries its tier (SRS section 6) and says whether a primary source would upgrade it. Two boxes are ticked: the second and the fifth, which Clark and von Euler hold whole. The first lacks a hypoxic step, the third the arms and the drive in sleep, the fourth the rows behind the rest's tidal volume and rate, and the sixth a measured bound on depth under a chemical drive.

### Rate and tidal volume over hypercapnic and hypoxic steps from rest

Hypercapnia, as a ramp and not a step: Clark and von Euler 1972 (primary, 7 healthy men, hyperoxic rebreathing to an end-tidal CO2 of 8%, spirometer, pp. 269 to 270). Tidal volume rose with the rate held until it reached 1.5 to 2 times its resting value (range 1, p. 267), then rate rose with it along Table 1's range 2 (p. 288). Ventilation in range 1 is the resting rate times tidal volume; in range 2 it is `V_T (V_T - V_0) / (C_h (1 + n) + (V_T - V_0) p)` (p. 288). One man's ventilation reached about 1.8 L/s against an inspiratory volume near 2.7 L (Fig. 10a, p. 288, read from the plot). The study reports the range constants and one man's plots, not a table of every man's rate and tidal volume. It differs from Hey 1966 on the first part: Clark and von Euler say Hey's subjects showed no range 1, their rate rising as soon as ventilation rose, and put it down to Hey's runs starting above rest (p. 293). That is Clark and von Euler's statement of another work, not a reading of Hey, which cannot be had. Rebreathing from oxygen keeps the peripheral arm near silent, so the figures are likely the central arm's under a hyperoxic ramp (inferred).

Hypoxia: missing. No work on hand gives rate and tidal volume under a hypoxic step. Van den Bosch 2021 (a review, p. 8) gives only the variability of ventilation and tidal volume under isocapnic hypoxia. Dahan 1990 recorded both breath by breath under end-tidal CO2 steps in nine resting men and published ventilation only. A primary study of rate and tidal volume under an isocapnic hypoxic step would close the box.

### Whether inspiration's length or expiration's carries the rate change

Both, with expiration following inspiration. Clark and von Euler 1972 (primary, man, hyperoxic rebreathing): above range 1, inspiration's length shortens as tidal volume rises (p. 280), and expiration's length is linear in inspiration's, `T_E = n T_I + p`, with `n` usually 0.5 to 1.0 and `p` close to zero in man (p. 283; one man's `n` 1.02, Fig. 9a, p. 287). In range 1, both lengths stay at their rest values as a cluster on the same line (p. 283). This is the parallel fall that Douglas 1982 cites them for (PDF p. 4), and it matches the sleep studies' unchanged share at rest (Douglas PDF p. 4; Stradling PDF pp. 3 to 4).

With `p` near zero the share `T_I / T_TOT` is `1 / (1 + n)` in both ranges: 0.5 to 0.67 by Clark and von Euler's `n`. That is above the share at rest in the works on hand: 0.35 to 0.37 in Stradling's six men (p. 366) and a median of 0.41 in Motamedi-Fakhr's 31 seated adults (p. 6). Clark and von Euler did not report the share, and the gap may come from their standing men breathing oxygen through a spirometer at raised drive. Harbour 2022 (a review, exercise, p. 4) gives the share rising from about 40% at rest to 50% or more at maximal exercise. So the share may rise with the drive. The default keeps it fixed at the rest's, which keeps the engine's one phase speed and is the nearest the works give at rest. A primary study that reports `T_I / T_TOT` under a CO2 step from rest would settle it.

### What bounds depth, and where rate takes the rest

Where rate joins: at a tidal volume of 1.5 to 2 times its resting value, the end of range 1 (Clark and von Euler, Summary 5, p. 267; primary, 7 healthy men, hyperoxic rebreathing). The extent varied between men, and those who breathed fast at rest had a longer range 1 (pp. 280 to 281). The textbooks' inflation-reflex volumes, 1 L (West PDF p. 321) and about 1.5 L (Guyton 12e p. 506), are no longer the bend: Clark and von Euler measured where inspiration starts to shorten in man.

What bounds depth: nothing in range 2. There depth and rate rise together, depth as about the square root of the drive (the form below). One man's run in Clark and von Euler reached about 4 L in inspiratory volume, with the range 3 group's mean near 3.5 L (Fig. 6, pp. 280 to 281, read from the plot); range 3 is a variable extra rise (p. 281). Tidal volume and inspiratory volume were equal up to 3 or 4 times the resting tidal volume and differed by up to about 15% above it (p. 282). The only plateau the works on hand give is in exercise: tidal volume generally peaks around 50 to 60% of vital capacity, 1.9 to 2.7 L (Harbour 2022, a review, p. 3, citing Blackie 1991), and plateaus after about 55% of vital capacity (Chuang 2019 p. 1, citing Wasserman's textbook). Chuang's 19 older men reached 1.97 L, 0.57 of their slow vital capacity, at peak exercise (Table 2, p. 5). Neither restates Hey 1966, and exercise is a non-goal, so these give a likely magnitude for a bound and not a source for one under a chemical drive. Hey 1966, which the leads say finds tidal volume linear in ventilation up to about half the vital capacity under CO2 and hypoxia, would give it. It cannot be had now, and the two later works said to restate it could not be fetched (Gaps). The form below holds no ceiling. Over the drives body 38 runs, from sleep to a 10% rise in production, the drive scale stays below a bend of 1.5, in range 1, so depth carries the change and the rate moves only by the smooth maximum's width (inferred).

### The split as a law can state it

From Clark and von Euler's Table 1 (p. 288), with one assumption from their model: the drive sets the speed at which the lung fills, mean inspiratory flow `V_T / T_I` (p. 268, Summary 9; p. 293). Douglas 1982 takes the same flow as the index of inspiratory drive (PDF p. 4, citing Milic-Emili and Grunstein 1976). With `p` near zero in man (p. 283), ventilation is that flow times the fixed share `1 / (1 + n)`, so the drive scale `C` is ventilation over its rest's in both ranges. The algebra below is inferred from Table 1, labeled so, and checked numerically against a direct solve of the table's equations.

With `C` the drive scale (dimensionless, its admitted beat's mean one at the awake rest), the rate factor `R` and the depth factor `D` are:

- `D(C) = C` and `R(C) = 1` up to the bend, `C ≤ k_V` (range 1: inspiration's length fixed, p. 288).
- Above the bend, `D(C) = (v_0 + sqrt(v_0^2 + 4 (k_V - v_0) C)) / 2` and `R(C) = C / D(C)` (range 2). This is Table 1's hyperbola `(V_T - V_0) T_I = C_h` in the resting breath's units: `D` is `V_T` over the resting tidal volume, `1 / R` is `T_I` over its rest's, `v_0` is `V_0` over the resting tidal volume, and continuity at the bend fixes `C_h` over the resting tidal volume times the resting `T_I` to `k_V - v_0`. With `v_0` zero, `D = sqrt(k_V C)` and `R = sqrt(C / k_V)`.
- `R(C) D(C) = C` holds in both ranges.
- For the engine, one form: `R(C) = smooth_max(1, C / D_2(C), w_V)`, the contract's smooth maximum, with `D_2` the range 2 expression, and `D(C) = C / R(C)`. Below the bend `C / D_2(C)` is under one, so the smooth maximum tends to one and `D` to `C`; above it `R` tends to `C / D_2(C)`. For a positive width the smooth maximum exceeds both operands, so `R` is above one at every `C`, by `w_V / 2` at the bend, and `D` is under both `C` and `D_2(C)`. At the bend, `D_2(k_V) = k_V` exactly when `k_V >= v_0 / 2`, which `k_V > v_0` gives. As `C` tends to zero, `D_2` tends to `v_0`, so `C / D_2` tends to zero for a positive `v_0` (and to zero as `sqrt(C / k_V)` for `v_0` zero), `R` to `(1 + sqrt(1 + w_V^2)) / 2` and `D` to zero. `D` is never negative. The drive scale is strictly positive at this head (its admissible set in `crates/ng-nervous/src/chemoreflex.rs`), so `C = 0` is a limit only.
- `D_2` needs a square root, and the contract's `Op` enum (`crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs` from line 310) holds `Exp` and `Ln` but no square root or power. Its argument `v_0^2 + 4 (k_V - v_0) C` is positive for every positive `C`, so the law can state the root as `exp(ln(x) / 2)`, or the builder adds a square root. Which one is body 38's call.
- Ventilation is linear in `C` only where both factors read the same `C` and tidal volume follows `D(C)` exactly, so it is no expectation for a fixture: the pump answers its drive through the junction's receptor and the airway, and depth set at a breath edge reads a different `C` from the rate's live one. Body 11's own offline calculation is the example: there a rate scale of 1.10 moves minute ventilation by 1.0801, since tidal volume falls with the shorter inspiration.

### What body 38 should know

The engine today, in `crates/ng-nervous/src/breath.rs`:

- The drive scale speeds the phase in both inspiration and expiration: line 103, `k.scale(rate, scale)`, inside the loop over `[&inspiration, &expiration]` at line 100. The guards end each phase at a fixed phase distance (`ns.inspiration-length` and `ns.expiration-length`, lines 56 and 58), so one phase speed fixes the two phases' distances, not the share of elapsed time. The share stays fixed for a drive held constant over the breath, which the sleep studies support at rest and Clark and von Euler's `T_E = n T_I` with `p` near zero supports under rebreathing. A drive higher through inspiration than through expiration shortens inspiration's time more and lowers its share, and the reverse raises it: with the packet's distances 0.4 and 0.6, a speed doubled through inspiration alone gives a share of 0.25 against 0.4.
- The pump drive rises toward `ns.drive-peak` (line 59) at the fixed `ns.drive-rise` (line 50, the rising law at lines 88 to 93) and falls at `ns.drive-fall` (line 51, lines 94 to 98). A shorter inspiration reaches a lower drive, so depth falls slightly as rate rises.

For depth to rise with the drive, the builder has two candidates. Clark and von Euler's model is the rise: the drive sets how fast the lung fills, and the end of inspiration, fixed in range 1 and on the volume and time hyperbola in range 2, sets depth (p. 268; p. 293). In the engine that is `ns.drive-rise` times `C`, with the phase speed times `R(C)` ending inspiration sooner, so that depth comes to `C / R(C)`. Eckert (PDF p. 565) and Ganong (PDF p. 1503) give the same form at textbook tier, from animals. The other candidate is the peak, `ns.drive-peak` times `D(C)` at each breath edge, native-contract 87's candidate. The peak is not the source's mechanism, and gives the source's depth only where the drive reaches its peak within inspiration.
