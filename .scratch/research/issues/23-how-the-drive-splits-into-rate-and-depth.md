## Answer

One primary study on hand gives the split under a rising chemical drive in man: Clark and von Euler 1972, seven healthy men rebreathing from a bell of oxygen, a hypercapnic ramp under hyperoxia. Up to a tidal volume of typically 1.5 to 2 times its resting value, inspiration's length stays fixed, so depth carries the rise and the rate stays at rest (range 1). Above that volume inspiration shortens as a hyperbola of tidal volume and expiration follows it, so depth and rate rise together (range 2), over the volumes their fit covered. They also state that Hey 1966 used a linear model of ventilation against tidal volume (p. 291). The Answer writes each as a form a law can state, inferred from the source with model choices the source does not make (The split as a law can state it). Body 38 takes Clark and von Euler's ranges form as a model default. It replaces the earlier model default, which put a ceiling on depth at the textbooks' inflation-reflex volume. The table below gives the tier of each work it lists, claim by claim where one work holds claims of different strength. An inference has no tier and is labeled.

### Rate and tidal volume over hypercapnic and hypoxic steps from rest

Hypercapnia, as a ramp and not a step: Clark and von Euler 1972 (primary, 7 healthy men, hyperoxic rebreathing until moderate discomfort or an end-tidal CO2 of 8%, spirometer, pp. 269 to 270). Tidal volume rose with the rate held until it reached typically 1.5 to 2 times its resting value (range 1, p. 267), then rate rose with it along Table 1's range 2 (p. 288). Ventilation in range 1 is the resting rate times tidal volume; in range 2 it is `V_T (V_T - V_0) / (C_h (1 + n) + (V_T - V_0) p)` (p. 288). In one man's plot of ventilation against inspiratory volume the highest point lies near 1.75 L/s at about 2.6 L (Fig. 10a, p. 288, read from the plot). The study reports the range constants and one man's plots, not a table of every man's rate and tidal volume. Of Hey 1966 it reports examples in which rate appeared to rise at once, with a raised starting ventilation as a likely explanation, and not a settled one (pp. 293 to 294, the table above). The runs measured the whole response under hyperoxia and do not isolate an arm.

Hypoxia: no work on hand gives rate and tidal volume under a hypoxic step. Van den Bosch 2021 (a review, p. 8) gives only the variability of ventilation and tidal volume under isocapnic hypoxia.

### Whether inspiration's length or expiration's carries the rate change

Both, with expiration following inspiration, over the rebreathing ranges Clark and von Euler measured. Clark and von Euler 1972 (primary, man, hyperoxic rebreathing): above range 1, inspiration's length shortens as tidal volume rises (p. 280), and expiration's length is linear in inspiration's, `T_E = n T_I + p`, with `n` usually 0.5 to 1.0 and `p` close to zero in man (p. 283; one man's `n` 1.02, Fig. 9a, p. 286, its caption p. 287). In range 1, both lengths stay at their rest values as a cluster on the same line (p. 283). This is the parallel fall that Douglas 1982 cites them for (PDF p. 4), and it matches the sleep studies' unchanged share at rest (Douglas PDF p. 4; Stradling PDF pp. 3 to 4).

Inspiration's share of the breath, from `T_E = n T_I + p`, is `T_I / T_TOT = 1 / (1 + n + p / T_I)`. It is fixed only where `p` is zero, at `1 / (1 + n)`. With `p` kept it moves as `T_I` does: at `n` 1 and `p` 0.1 s it is 0.484 at a `T_I` of 1.5 s and 0.469 at 0.75 s. Setting `p` to zero is a model choice, since Clark and von Euler give `p` only as close to zero in man (p. 283), and it gives 0.5 to 0.67 by their `n`. The share at rest in the works on hand is lower: 0.367 awake in Stradling's six men (p. 366) and a median of 0.41 in Motamedi-Fakhr's 31 seated adults (p. 6). Clark and von Euler did not report the share. These are different people, postures and stimuli, so the gap does not show the share rising with the drive in one person. Harbour 2022 (a review, exercise, p. 4) gives a rise from about 40% at rest to 50% or more at maximal exercise, from the works it cites. The default holds the share at the rest's, which keeps the engine's one phase speed. A primary study that reports `T_I / T_TOT` under a CO2 step from rest would settle it.

### The arms and sleep

The central and the peripheral arm: no source on hand separates their split, by the search above. Clark and von Euler measured the whole response under hyperoxia, and hyperoxia does not silence the peripheral arm. Dahan 1990 (J Physiol 428:485, primary, nine healthy men aged 20 to 26, seated, end-tidal CO2 steps by dynamic end-tidal forcing) partitioned the breath-by-breath response into a fast and a slow component (Summary 3), argued that the fast one is the peripheral loop and the slow one the central (Summary 5), found the fast component under hyperoxia averaging about 24% of the total CO2 sensitivity in three of nine men, absent in three and doubtful in three (Summary 4), and concludes that the peripheral chemoreceptors in general add to ventilation under hyperoxia (Summary 8; all printed p. 485). Dahan recorded tidal volume, inspiratory and expiratory time, rate and ventilation breath by breath (printed p. 487) and publishes the arms' responses in ventilation only. The default applies one split to the summed drive scale, as the body sums the arms today.

Waking against NREM sleep: at rest on room air, tidal volume was lower asleep in all three studies, significantly in Douglas and in Gothe's stage II and not significantly in Stradling's 6 men; the change in rate was small and its direction differs by study (the table above, primary). These are stage means, not a drive moved in one person. Under CO2 in sleep, Gothe's abstract gives ventilation's response at 79% of waking on average and a smaller rise in mean inspiratory flow, and no rate or tidal volume, so no work on hand measures the split under a chemical step in sleep.

In the engine, sleep is not a drive scale set under one. The code computes `C = (rest - W s) + rising - falling`. With both gains zero, asleep it is the rounded difference `rest - W s`. Below one, the ranges form moves depth and holds the rate at rest but for the width's effect, and the linear form with `h_V` between zero and one moves both.

### What bounds depth, and where rate takes the rest

Where rate joins, under the ranges form: at a tidal volume of typically 1.5 to 2 times its resting value, the end of range 1 (Clark and von Euler, Summary 5, p. 267; primary, 7 healthy men, hyperoxic rebreathing). The extent varied between men, and those who breathed fast at rest had a longer range 1 (pp. 280 to 281). The textbooks' inflation-reflex volumes, 1 L (West PDF p. 321) and about 1.5 L (Guyton 12e p. 506), are not the bend: Clark and von Euler measured where inspiration starts to shorten in man. That bend is the hard form's: under a positive width the engine's law already moves the rate below it (The split as a law can state it).

What bounds depth: no work on hand measures a bound under a chemical drive. In range 2 depth and rate rise together, each as the square root of the drive at `a_V` zero (below). Range 2 was fitted to group means of inspiratory volume up to about 3.1 L in Fig. 6b's man, with the uppermost group, near 3.6 L, left out as range 3, and that man's single breaths reached about 4 L (Fig. 6, pp. 280 to 281, read from the plot). ### The split as a law can state it

Model choices, none made by the source, carry both forms to the engine's drive scale `C` (dimensionless, its admitted beat's mean one at the awake rest). Each is an inference, and the forms below name the ones they use:

- **Choice 1, the drive's measure.** The ranges form takes `C` as mean inspiratory flow `V_T / T_I` over its rest's. Clark and von Euler's model has the drive set how fast the lung fills (p. 268, Summary 9; p. 293), and Douglas 1982 calls the same flow an index of inspiratory drive (PDF pp. 1 and 2), but neither calibrates the engine's `C` to it. The linear form takes `C` as ventilation over its rest's.
- **Choice 2, `p` zero.** With `p` kept, inspiration's share is `1 / (1 + n + p / T_I)` (above), the rate factor is not the rest's `T_I` over `T_I`, and ventilation is not mean inspiratory flow times a fixed share. The ranges form sets `p` to zero, so the share is `1 / (1 + n)` in both ranges, the rate factor is the rest's `T_I` over `T_I`, and `C` is also ventilation over its rest's. The linear form needs no timing law and so no `p`.
- **Choice 3, the linear form's line through rest.** Below.

`R` is the rate factor and `D` the depth factor, each over the awake rest's, and `R D = C` in both forms in real arithmetic.

**The ranges form, Clark and von Euler's** (inferred from Table 1, p. 288, with choices 1 and 2):

- `D(C) = C` and `R(C) = 1` up to the bend, `C <= b_V` (range 1: inspiration's length fixed).
- Above the bend, `D(C) = (a_V + sqrt(a_V^2 + 4 (b_V - a_V) C)) / 2` and `R(C) = C / D(C)` (range 2). This is Table 1's hyperbola `(V_T - V_0) T_I = C_h` in the resting breath's units: `D` is `V_T` over the resting tidal volume, `1 / R` is `T_I` over its rest's, `a_V` is `V_0` over the resting tidal volume, and continuity at the bend fixes `C_h` over the resting tidal volume times the resting `T_I` to `b_V - a_V`. With `a_V` zero, `D = sqrt(b_V C)` and `R = sqrt(C / b_V)`. Both factors are exactly one at rest, and `D_2(b_V) = b_V` for every `a_V` under `b_V`.
- It holds over the volumes range 2 was fitted on. Above them, and through range 3, it is an extrapolation (above).
- For the engine, `R(C) = smooth_max(1, C / D_2(C), w_V)`, the contract's smooth maximum, with `D_2` the range 2 expression, and `D(C) = C / R(C)`. `D_2` needs a square root: the contract's `Op` enum (`crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs` from line 310) holds `Exp`, `Ln`, `SmoothMax` and `SmoothMin` but no square root, power or hard maximum. The law can state the root of `x = a_V^2 + 4 (b_V - a_V) C`, positive for every positive `C`, as `exp(ln(x) / 2)`, or the builder adds a square root. Which one is body 38's call.
- The width moves both factors. In real arithmetic the smooth maximum exceeds both operands for a positive width, so `R` is above one at every `C` and `D` is under both `C` and `D_2(C)`. `D` and `R` are then factors on the hard form's rest, not on the smooth form's own. At `b_V` 1.5, `a_V` 0 and `w_V` 0.2: `R(1)` is 1.04396 and `D(1)` 0.95789, and at the bend `R` is `1 + w_V / 2`, 1.1, and `D(1.5)` is 1.36364, not 1.5. Below the bend `R` tends to one only as `w_V` tends to zero: at a fixed width, as `C` tends to zero, `R` tends to `(1 + sqrt(1 + w_V^2)) / 2`, and `D` to zero. So `b_V` is the hard form's bend, and `b_V` and `a_V` fitted against a measured tidal volume ratio are fitted against the form with its width.
- In binary64, at `b_V` 1.5 and `a_V` 0, with the contract's smooth maximum (`crates/ng-contract/src/evaluate.rs`, lines 34 to 42) and `D_2`'s root taken as a correctly rounded square root (through `exp(ln(x) / 2)` the builder measures them): a width up to about 6.7e-9 returns `R` and `D` exactly one at `C = 1`, and a width up to about 2.2e-16 returns `R` exactly one at `C = 1.5`. At a width of 6.7e-9, `R` is 1.0000000000000002, one unit in the last place above one, at `C = 1.0266`, 11b's breath mean at one minute (body 11, line 71), and 1.00000000335 at the bend. Each holds at its own drive only.

**The linear form, Hey 1966's relation** (inferred from Clark and von Euler's statement of it, p. 291, with choice 1 as ventilation and choice 3):

- Clark and von Euler report only that Hey and colleagues used a linear model of ventilation against tidal volume (p. 291). Written `V_E = m (V_T - k)`, with `m` a rate and `k` a volume. Choice 3 takes the line through the resting pair, resting ventilation and resting tidal volume. For a line fitted independently, the coefficient of `C` is `V_E,rest / (m V_T,rest)`. It equals `1 - h_V` when the line passes through the resting pair. With `C` the ventilation over its rest's and `h_V` the intercept `k` over the resting tidal volume, `D(C) = h_V + (1 - h_V) C` and `R(C) = C / D(C)`. Both are exactly one at rest.
- `h_V` lies in `[0, 1)`: under one, since a rising line through rest at a positive resting ventilation meets zero below the resting tidal volume, and at zero or above for depth to stay positive at every positive drive. At `h_V` zero, `D = C` and the rate never moves. For `h_V` between zero and one both move: the slope of `R` at rest is `h_V`, at low drive `D` tends to `h_V` and `R` to zero, and at high drive `R` tends to `1 / (1 - h_V)` while depth grows without bound.
