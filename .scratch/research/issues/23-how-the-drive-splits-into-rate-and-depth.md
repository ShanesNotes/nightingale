The three sleep studies differ on rate at rest: Douglas found it slightly faster in every stage, Stradling found no significant change, and Gothe's abstract gives a fall of one breath a minute. Stradling's Discussion (printed pp. 368 to 369) suggests a reason, as a possibility and not a measurement: Douglas used a tight mask at 1,600 m, with mild hypoxaemia (end-tidal PO2 about 84 mmHg) and about 80 mL of added dead space, and masks are known to slow the waking rate, an effect that might vanish with sleep. The body takes Stradling's: it used no mask and no altitude, and the body's patient breathes through no mask; Gothe's canopy, also without a mask, gives a rate within one breath a minute of waking. All three find tidal volume lower asleep. Douglas and Stradling find inspiration's share unchanged. Douglas finds mean inspiratory flow lower in REM, and Stradling's fall in it is significant only from waking to REM. A primary study of sleep without a mask, in more subjects and both sexes, would upgrade it.

### Whether inspiration's length or expiration's carries the rate change

Partly answered, for sleep only. Douglas 1982 found a faster rate asleep carried by parallel falls in `T_I` and `T_E`, with `T_I / T_TOT` unchanged (primary, resting sleep, PDF p. 4). The same page says such parallel falls also occur during carbon dioxide rebreathing, citing Clark and von Euler 1972: a statement that cites another work, not a measurement in this study, so it supports the model default and does not hold the box. Stradling 1985 found `T_I / T_TOT` unchanged across waking and sleep (primary, PDF pp. 3 to 4). Eckert gives the constant ratio as a mammalian rule (textbook, mismatched population). No work on hand tests it under a chemical step, so the model default below keeps inspiration's share fixed and lets both lengths carry the rate. Clark and von Euler 1972 (J Physiol 222:267 to 295, locator checked in Douglas 1982's reference list) would settle how `T_I` falls once tidal volume reaches its off-switch volume.

### What bounds depth, and where rate takes the rest

Model default, by inference from textbook figures. No work on hand measures where depth stops rising under a chemical drive. The textbooks give the volume at which the inflation reflex acts: largely inactive in adult humans unless tidal volume exceeds 1 L (West PDF p. 321), and probably not active until tidal volume passes about three times normal, about 1.5 L (Guyton 12e p. 506). Both are textbook tier, adult humans, with no method stated. They disagree on what the reflex then does to timing: West gives a slower rate from a longer expiration, and Guyton a sooner end to inspiration and so a faster rate. Taking the reflex's volume as depth's ceiling under a chemical drive, with rate carrying the rise above it, is an inference from these lines. The figures put the ceiling at 1 to 1.5 L: two to three times a normal breath of about 500 mL (West PDF p. 30), or 2.4 to 3.6 times Stradling's waking 420 mL. Hey 1966 would give the measured volume where rate takes over, and Clark and von Euler 1972 the form of the bend.

### The split as a law can state it

Model default with no sourced form, from the rows above. With `C` the drive scale (dimensionless, its admitted beat's mean one at the awake rest), the rate factor `R` and the depth factor `D` are:

- `R(C) = smooth_max(1, C / k_V, w_V)`, the contract's smooth maximum, `(1 + C / k_V + sqrt((1 - C / k_V)^2 + w_V^2)) / 2`. The phase's speed is `ns.breath-rate` times `R(C)`.
- `D(C) = C / R(C)`. The pump drive's peak is `ns.drive-peak` times `D(C)`.
- The branches are limits. Far below the bend, `C / k_V` many widths under one, `R` tends to one and `D` to `C`; far above it, `R` tends to `C / k_V` and `D` to `k_V`. For a positive width the smooth maximum exceeds both operands, so `R` is above one at every `C` and `D` is under both `C` and `k_V`: depth approaches the ceiling from below, and rate already moves under the bend. At the bend, `C = k_V`, `R` is `1 + w_V / 2` and `D` is `k_V / (1 + w_V / 2)`. As `C` tends to zero, `R` tends to `(1 + sqrt(1 + w_V^2)) / 2` and `D` to zero. `D` is never negative, where `smooth_min(C, k_V, w_V)` with the contract's smooth minimum would fall below zero for `C` under `w_V^2 / (4 k_V)`. The drive scale is strictly positive at this head (its admissible set in `crates/ng-nervous/src/chemoreflex.rs`), so `C = 0` is a limit only.
- `R(C) D(C) = C` is an identity of the two factors. Ventilation is linear in `C` only where both factors read the same `C` and tidal volume follows `D(C)` exactly, so it is no expectation for a fixture: the pump answers its drive through the junction's receptor and the airway, and depth set at a breath edge reads a different `C` from the rate's live one. Body 11's own offline calculation is the example: there a rate scale of 1.10 moves minute ventilation by 1.0801, since tidal volume falls with the shorter inspiration.

Where this default may be wrong: it holds the rate fixed until depth nears its ceiling, and no source on hand tests that. A study of rate against tidal volume under a rising drive is the first thing to read when the works arrive, since it decides whether rate rises with depth from rest.

The law scales two parameters the breath reads today, `ns.drive-peak` and `ns.breath-rate`. Resting tidal volume and resting rate are not parameters: they are what the body shows at rest, produced by the pump and the phase, and SRS invariant I14 keeps a produced quantity out of the parameters. Research 20's measurements of them are what the resting peak and phase speed are fitted against, and today's rate is `ns.breath-rate` over `ns.inspiration-length` plus `ns.expiration-length`.

The range: from below the awake rest (sleep, `C < 1`) up to drives above the bend. In sleep the form puts the whole fall in depth with rate held at its rest: that is the default's own simplification, nearest Stradling's resting figures, while the three sleep studies give a rate slightly faster (Douglas), unchanged (Stradling) or slightly slower (Gothe) than waking. ### What body 38 should know

The engine today, in `crates/ng-nervous/src/breath.rs`:

- The drive scale speeds the phase in both inspiration and expiration: line 103, `k.scale(rate, scale)`, inside the loop over `[&inspiration, &expiration]` at line 100. The guards end each phase at a fixed phase distance (`ns.inspiration-length` and `ns.expiration-length`, lines 56 and 58), so one phase speed fixes the two phases' distances, not the share of elapsed time. The share stays fixed for a drive held constant over the breath, which the sleep studies support at rest. A drive higher through inspiration than through expiration shortens inspiration's time more and lowers its share, and the reverse raises it: with the packet's distances 0.4 and 0.6, a speed doubled through inspiration alone gives a share of 0.25 against 0.4.
- The pump drive rises toward `ns.drive-peak` (line 59) at the fixed `ns.drive-rise` (line 50, the rising law at lines 88 to 93) and falls at `ns.drive-fall` (line 51, lines 94 to 98). A shorter inspiration reaches a lower drive, so depth falls slightly as rate rises.

For depth to rise with the drive, the builder changes the peak: `ns.drive-peak` times `D(C)` at each breath edge, native-contract 87's candidate. The rate keeps its form with `R(C)` in place of `C` on line 103. A competing form is on hand: the drive raises the rate of rise of inspiratory activity, and vagal feedback ends inspiration without changing that rate (Eckert PDF p. 565 and Ganong PDF p. 1503, textbook tier, from animals). In the engine that form is `ns.drive-rise` times `D(C)`. Both raise mean inspiratory flow at a fixed inspiration's length, which is what the sleep studies read as the drive. The peak is the pick: the drive relaxes toward its peak, so a faster rise moves depth less and less as the drive nears the peak within inspiration, while the peak sets the level the drive reaches, and native-contract 87's firing sets one memory once a breath. A primary study of the drive's time course under CO2 would settle between them. Scaling both `ns.drive-rise` and `ns.drive-fall` by `R(C)` keeps the neural drive's waveform in phase at a held peak. It does not keep tidal volume: transmitter release, reuptake and binding at the pump's junction, filling through the airway's conductance and the recoil all run in elapsed time, so a faster waveform can give a smaller swing in volume (the packet's breath calculation steps them in time, `crates/ng-validate/src/body/packet.rs` from line 1406). What depth does as rate rises is read on the fixture.
