# 13: Law forms for the gut and the fuels

Type: research
Status: resolved
Claimed by: r13-gut-fuels
Blocked by: 02

**Question:** Which cited form does each law that carries a meal from the gut to the stores take at rung 0? [Body tickets 13 to 16](../../body/map.md) write the laws, so each answer is a form, where its constants go, a stated validity range and how far the source was read, to the standard of [ticket 02](02-law-forms.md). No value is bound here.

- [x] Gastric emptying and absorption by segment, for water, sodium, potassium, chloride, glucose and amino acids
- [x] Dietary fat by the lymph route
- [x] The liver as the glucose hub: glycogen storage and release, glucose output against insulin and glucagon, and glucose made overnight from lactate, amino acids and glycerol
- [x] Urea synthesis from amino acids, so nitrogen in equals nitrogen out over a day
- [x] Insulin and glucagon secretion against glucose, with portal and systemic concentrations, and their clearance
- [x] Glucose uptake by muscle and fat against insulin, and the potassium that moves into cells with it
- [x] Fat storage after a meal and release overnight, and ketones made by the liver and burned by the tissues
- [x] Body protein turnover at its smallest, and creatinine production from muscle
- [x] The kidney's handling of each new solute: glucose reclaimed up to a limit, urea partly reclaimed, creatinine filtered
- [x] Diet-induced heat production
- [x] A validity range for each form, with its source

## Answer

Each row is a form, where its constants go and where it holds, to the standard of [ticket 02](02-law-forms.md). Constants become records under [Parameters](05-parameters.md), and none is bound here. `Holds for` cites a source where one states a range and says "model default" where none was found. "Model adaptation" means a sourced form was changed to fit the contract or the anatomy, and the row says how. "Model default" in a form means no source on hand states that law, and the section "No form on hand" names the search. Every source except Schaller's supplement, whose rows say how it was read, was read in the evidence store's extracted text, at the pages, equations or tables named. The store holds the PDF text layer of books and older papers, and the PubMed Central XML or HTML of newer ones. "Full text" means the whole work was on hand and the cited parts were read. No page cited here is one the store marks as OCR. Where the store's text garbles an equation, the row says how it was read. Pages are printed pages unless a row says PDF page. A work named only as another work cites it was not read. The rows are grouped by the body ticket that writes them. The curation fills, slice D of the healthy-body draft and the HumMod model files were leads and are cited nowhere.

| Gastric emptying | Each species leaves the stomach lumen for the small intestine's at `J_i = q(K) n_i / (V_h + V)`, with `n_i` its amount in the stomach and `V` the stomach's water volume. `q(K) = 1 / (2 (a - b exp(-K)))` is the volume efflux in mL/min, and `K = E / (V_h + V)` is the contents' nutritive density, with `E` their energy at 4 kcal/g for carbohydrate and protein and 9 kcal/g for fat. ### The interfaces with research 11, 12, 14 and 15

- **Research 11.** Its tissue respiration burns one mixed fuel with one respiratory quotient until ticket 16. The fuel mix row splits the same demand `D_bed` among glucose, fatty acids, lactate and ketones, so the quotient follows what is burned. Its lactate row makes lactate when oxygen falls short, and the fuel mix and gluconeogenesis rows use it.
- **Research 12.** The meal's heat arrives as the energy of the storage and conversion reactions, in each bed's thermal pool. Research 12's heat production after a meal reads that heat and adds no meal term of its own.
- **Research 14.** Insulin's factor `rho` scales the maximum rate of research 14's sodium pump, and potassium's excretion is research 14's. Stadt predicts that a signal from the gut, which raises the kidney's potassium secretion during a load, does most to hold the body's potassium over days, which is research 14's to weigh. The amino acid reaction's rate carries the fixed acid's sulfur that research 14's acid production reads. Absorbing sodium and chloride by separate laws moves the strong ion difference of the lumen and the plasma, which research 14's acid-base interface holds. The diet's organic anion and ammonium are research 14's.
- **Research 15.** No form on hand puts cortisol on the liver's output. Cortisol can raise gluconeogenesis 6- to 10-fold (Guyton p. 928). It raises the gluconeogenic enzyme PEPCK and frees amino acids from muscle protein (Guyton p. 928, Berne and Levy p. 776). So the glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis rows leave a gain for research 15, and so does muscle protein's breakdown, which body 16's constant rates cannot take. Albumin's synthesis and the minerals' absorption from the gut are research 15's.

### Decisions

- **Reactions, not an output law.** Body 01 makes glycogen a pool, and gluconeogenesis and urea reactions, so the liver's output is the sum of its reactions. Visentin's fitted production checks the release reactions alone, glycogen breakdown and gluconeogenesis, because it does not subtract the liver's uptake. An output law could neither fill nor empty the glycogen pool, and no output form on hand has a glucagon term.
- **The anatomy in place of each model's own pools.** The glucose and insulin models on hand put insulin's action and glucose in pools of their own: the minimal model's remote insulin `X`, the meal model's `X`, which it calls interstitial insulin, its delayed `I_d`, Visentin's two stages `X_1` and `X_P`, and the meal model's split of glucose into a plasma pool `G_p` and a slowly equilibrating `G_t`. Rung 0 reads each through the anatomy. Insulin crosses each bed's capillary wall into the interstitium and binds there through the receptor layer, and glucose sits in each bed's plasma, interstitium and cells. The lags the models fit become the wall's passage and the binding's rates, which tickets 15 and 16 calibrate against Visentin's fits. The liver's two-stage delay becomes one binding lag, so the delay's shape is not kept.
- **Static insulin secretion.** The first phase of secretion needs `dG/dt`. Rung 0 keeps the static part and cannot show the first phase.
- **Emptying from what the stomach holds.** Eqn 2's half-time is proportional to a meal's starting volume, so a rate set by density alone matches it without a record of when a meal began. The cost is the curve's shape, linear within a meal, whereas liquids empty close to exponentially (Hunt and Spurrell). An exponential with a declared reference volume in place of `V0` keeps that shape and gets the half-time right only at that volume. It is the fallback if body ticket 13's check on the half-time fails.
- **A fecal exit tied to absorption.** With no colon, a transit law would send the colon's water out. Tying the exit to absorption gives each meal's unabsorbed share and closes every conserved quantity.
- **One lumen for the small intestine and the colon.** Each absorption constant is a grouped value, so the differences between jejunum and ileum and the colon's water and salt absorption are folded in. A row that misses its check earns a split (record 0002).
- **No empty space is a refusal source.** The emptying row divides by `V_h + V` and the carrier rows by `K + c`, never by a volume that can reach zero. `ln` reads only urine flow in the urea row and glucose in the glucagon row, neither of which reaches zero on a healthy day.
- **No literal one.** The contract's only literal is zero (`crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs`), so each factor `1 + S E` or `1 - S E` is written distributed, as `k + k S E`, or takes its one from a declared parameter.
- **Van Slyke's flow law for urea.** A fixed share would misstate the night, when urine flow falls.
- **The meal's heat from the cost of storage.** It follows the fluxes in time and needs no law of its own. A run that misses D'Alessio's 8% earns the fixed-share alternative as its own oxidation reaction.
- **Muscle protein carries creatine.** Creatinine's production is proportional to muscle mass, so a form first order in muscle protein keeps its daily total without a new species.
- **Insulin's potassium shift is small.** Stadt's fit gives up to 10% more pump uptake at meal insulin, about 14 mEq of a meal's potassium into cells. Body ticket 15's check that a meal's potassium moves into cells first should expect a shift of that size.

### What the contract lacks

Each form above uses only the operators of instruction set v1. These gaps are new beyond research 11's list and body 01's table of what the contract lacks:

- No operator gives a rate of change. The dynamic insulin secretion (Seike, the meal-model family) and Visentin's portal term read `dG/dt` while glucose rises. A memory that relaxes toward glucose would give a filtered difference, `k (G - G_f)`, kept dimensionless against a declared reference as research 11 did for oxygen debt. Rung 0 leaves both terms out.
- No law can hold a value from the instant of a delivery. Hunt and Stubbs's half-time scales with each meal's starting volume, which a law could read only through a memory that jumps when a meal arrives. The emptying row does without it.
- Only metabolism may declare a reaction, at the capillary beds (`ReactsAt`, body 02). The liver's glycogen, gluconeogenesis, amino acid and ketone reactions need a grant for the hepatic card at the liver bed, and muscle's glycogen, protein and creatinine reactions one for the musculoskeletal card at the muscle bed (ticket 04).
- Body 01 measures glycogen, triglyceride and body protein as masses, but a reaction counts whole units of a fixed formula (body 02). Each needs a unit with atoms for its reactions: a glucosyl residue, a triglyceride of stated formula and an amino acid residue (ticket 03).
- The receptor layer binds a ligand in a junction's cleft or in plasma (`crates/ng-receptor/src/lib.rs`). The uptake and potassium rows read insulin bound in the interstitium of the muscle and fat beds, so the layer needs a third place to bind. Without it, ticket 15 reads insulin bound in the bed's plasma and puts the whole lag on binding.
