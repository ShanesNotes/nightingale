## Answer

Constants become records under [Parameters](05-parameters.md), and none is bound here. `Holds for` cites a source where one states a range and says "model default" where none was found. "Model adaptation" means a sourced form was changed to fit the contract, and the row says how. Every source was read in the evidence store's extracted text, at the pages or equations named. "Full text" means the whole work was on hand and the cited parts were read. "Abstract" means only the abstract was read, from Europe PMC. No page cited here is one the store marks as OCR. West's 11th edition sets its equations as images, which the store's text drops, so only its prose is cited. The curation fills and slice B of the healthy-body draft were leads and are cited nowhere.

### The interface with ticket 14

The gas loop and the acid loop meet at one algebraic row in each space. Ticket 14's charge balance sets [H+]. This ticket's CO2 content reads [H+] through `K1`, and its bicarbonate term is the one the charge balance counts, so both read one `K1` record. The oxygen saturation reads pH through `f_pH`. In blood, hemoglobin also binds protons more strongly when it gives up oxygen (Dash's `K5''` and `K6''`). That binding is the rest of the Haldane effect, and it is a weak-acid charge that ticket 14's balance carries if blood is one space. The curation fill for BC-L01 names albumin and phosphate only. Which ticket writes the hemoglobin charge term is ticket 14's to decide.

Ticket 08 cannot hold pH while it waits for ticket 18. With [H+] fixed at Dash's standard, his Table 1 gives a CO2 content slope near 0.5 mmol/L per mmHg. Guyton's curve at fixed PO2 gives 2 vol% over 5 mmHg, about 0.18 mmol/L per mmHg. Both slopes are arithmetic on the cited numbers. A body with pH held would store and release about three times too much CO2 for each mmHg, so ticket 08 needs the charge balance or a sourced buffer line.

### Decisions

- **Dash over Severinghaus.** Severinghaus 1979 is not on hand, so its form could not be checked. Dash puts each shift on P50 as its own factor and gives the content of both gases in one table. The cost is `ln`, which refuses a PO2 at or below zero. On a healthy day every space keeps some oxygen, so a refusal there marks a fault, not a state the milestone reaches.
- **The shifts rung 0 keeps.** pH, PCO2 and temperature each have a producer at rung 0: the acid, gas and heat loops. 2,3-DPG has none, so its factor stays at 1. Each factor is 1 at its standard.
- **Desaturation, not Weil's hyperbola.** The hyperbola's divisor reaches zero at 32 mmHg, and `divide` refuses a step whose divisor is not positive. Reading desaturation from the first row keeps the carotid body on oxygen tension, because the row maps PO2 to saturation whatever the hemoglobin. Weil reports that carbon monoxide, which lowers content and keeps tension, fails to stimulate breathing.
- **The controller's output is release.** Dahan's and Douglas's gains are ventilation per mmHg of the whole loop, pump and mechanics included. In the body the controller releases transmitter at the pump's junctions (body ticket 11). Its gains are calibrated until the resting loop gives the measured ventilation slopes.
- **No neural lag at rung 0.** Dahan's central time constant is about three times what cerebral blood flow predicts, and Dahan points to neuronal dynamics. Ticket 02 put no lag on an effector, and this ticket puts none on the central arm. A ventilation step response that misses its check earns one, through `relax`.
- **A linear store for tissue CO2.** A tissue charge balance would make the capacity follow from tissue buffers, but that balance lands with ticket 18, after ticket 09 needs the store. With ventilation in the loop, one store's time constant falls as alveolar ventilation rises. A fall in PCO2 after a rise in ventilation is then faster than a rise after a fall, the direction of Nunn's Figure 104 (half-times about 3 and 16 min). That is arithmetic on the form, not a source.
- **Ventilation drives airway water, not metabolic rate.** Stolwijk uses metabolic rate in place of ventilation. The body computes ventilation, so the loss follows the breath, and sleep or a held breath moves it. Stolwijk's 44 mmHg and Guyton's 47 mmHg differ in value only, which ticket 05 picks.
- **Tidal or quasi-steady.** Body ticket 10 prototypes both. A tidal law needs the dead space, the membrane and the gas fractions. A quasi-steady law needs the alveolar ventilation equation and the alveolar gas equation. If the airway space mixes its gas rather than returning it in order, it returns less than `V_D` of alveolar gas each breath, so ticket 10 states which it builds when it binds `V_D`.

### What the contract lacks

No form needs a new operator. Each uses `multiply`, `divide`, `scale`, `exp`, `ln`, `logistic`, `smooth_max`, `smooth_min` or `relax`. These gaps remain:

- The identity table has no temperature-over-temperature row, which the temperature terms of the first, second and fourth rows need. It has no Henry's-law row turning solubility times pressure into a concentration. Oxygen is a named dimension and not a substance, and carbon dioxide, hemoglobin and H+ are absent. Ticket 03 grows these lists.
- Memory takes only non-material dimensions, and an oxygen debt's natural unit is an amount of oxygen. Ticket 09 keeps the debt dimensionless against a declared reference amount, or ticket 03 adds a non-material debt dimension.
- `Sensed` has no concentration, so no set point can guard brain [H+]. If body ticket 11 makes the threshold a set point, the sensor reads brain pH, which is dimensionless.
- Powers of ten in `nH`, `R_rbc` and pH become `exp` and `ln`, with `ln 10` folded into a declared parameter.
- There is no delay operator. Dahan's transport delays drop, which body ticket 11 lists as a non-goal.

- **The vessels' answer to debt.** Searches for "oxygen debt" and "metabolic vasodilation" found Guyton 12e and 13e, Levick, Klabunde, Berne and Levy, Ganong and Eckert. None read gives an equation. The row above is a model default.
- **Dry heat of warming inspired air.** The search for "respiratory heat loss" found Stolwijk, which counts evaporation only, and Kingma 2014, whose respiratory term sits in a supplement not on hand.
- **Breathing rhythm.** No box covers how drive splits into rate and depth. Douglas 1982 (Thorax) and Gothe 1981 report both awake and asleep, and no form was sought.
