## Answer

Each row is a form, where its constants go and where it holds, to the standard of [ticket 02](02-law-forms.md). The rows are the ones [body ticket 01](../../body/issues/01-rung-0-specification.md) keeps for EN, RN, FL and BC, for NS's drive to vasopressin and for the fixed acid's terms, and no more. Constants become records under [Parameters](05-parameters.md), and none is bound here. `Holds for` cites a source where one states a range and says "model default" where none was found. "Model adaptation" means a sourced form was changed to fit the contract, and the row says how. `E_X` is ticket 02's receptor effect for receptor X, and every hormone's action below reads one.

Every source was read in the evidence store's extracted text at the pages, sections or equations named, with these exceptions. Hallow 2017's supplement and model code were read in their original files (`PSP4-6-383-s001.docx` and `-s002.txt`), which the store does not index. Robertson and Athar 1976 and George 1975 were read as abstracts, in the OpenAlex records the curation searches saved. "Full text" means the whole work was on hand and the cited parts were read. No page cited here is one the store marks as OCR. Robertson 1973, Baumann and Dingman 1976 and Edelman 1958 are old journal scans whose text layer has scanning errors, so only numbers that read cleanly are cited, and Edelman's table is cited by its terms only. Rose and Post 5e is a converted help file whose text drops characters at line ends, so it is cited by its print-page markers. The MathML of Karaaslan 2014 and Hallow 2017 is flattened in the text, so their forms are given by structure, and Hallow's Eq A12 was read in the DOCX's math markup. The curation fills and the healthy-body draft were leads and are cited nowhere.

### Decisions

- **The receptor layer carries every hormone's action.** As in ticket 02, an effect `E` from receptor occupancy replaces each source's curve of hormone concentration. It replaces Karaaslan's sympathetic logistic, Hallow's AT1 power law and bound-hormone logistics, and the Guyton report's angiotensin and aldosterone multipliers. A feedback written `1 - S E` with `S < 1` stays positive, while Hallow's power law needs `ln` of the bound amount, which refuses at zero.
- **Renin reads no pressure directly.** Karaaslan leaves a direct pressure effect out, citing work that found it negligible beside the macula densa's (Block 36). Pressure reaches renin through the macula densa's sodium and through sympathetic traffic. Beard 2013 Eq 19 competes, with renin falling as a tanh of time-averaged arterial pressure, fitted to rabbits in graded hemorrhage.
- **Potassium alone sets aldosterone's second term.** Karaaslan's Eq A76 and Stadt 2022's Eq 21 read the ratio of potassium to sodium concentration. That ratio divides two species' concentrations, and the identity table holds a concentration only over the same species' concentration. The Guyton report's AL4 reads potassium alone, as Guyton and Hall's Fig. 29-5 does. Karaaslan's arterial pressure term (Eq A77) drops, because pressure reaches aldosterone through renin and angiotensin II.
- **Volume moves vasopressin's threshold, not its slope.** Robertson and Athar's three lines have slopes from 0.31 to 0.35 and differ in threshold. Ganong's Fig. 38-4 competes. In 10 normal men the slope was 0.52 in hypovolemia against 0.38 in hypervolemia, with the intercept further left. The Guyton report instead adds a pressure term to an osmotic term (AD1 to AD9). One shift parameter is the smallest form that lets volume move the drive.
- **The night is the clock's.** George's subjects lay recumbent day and night, so posture does not explain their rise, and the rise was progressive through the night in 5 of them. With a plasma half-life of 24.1 min, a step at sleep onset would settle within about two hours, so a cosine of the clock fits a progressive rise better than the sleep state does. That is arithmetic on the cited numbers.
- **Pressure natriuresis stays a check.** Beard 2013 Eqs 21 and 22 give urine output as a law of mean pressure above an offset that sympathetic tone and angiotensin II move, capped at 10 ml/min. Ticket 17's run needs night urine to fall with no law setting urine flow, and its pressure check needs the answer to come from reclaim. So Hallow's factor on reclaim is taken.
- **Delivery enters potassium secretion.** The box asks for it. The Guyton report's form carries delivery (KD88 to KD90), and Stadt's has no flow term.
- **The pump saturates in extracellular potassium.** Stadt fits its parameters for a man, and the saturation keeps a meal's potassium from entering cells faster than `V_max` allows. Clegg 2023 competes, moving potassium between fluid spaces by linear rate constants with no pump. With the pump's rate set by potassium, cell sodium settles where its leak matches 1.5 times the pump.
- **Cell water moves by a flux.** Boron's Eq 5-27 gives the equilibrium, which one algebraic row per bed could hold. Water in cells is an amount, so a transfer moves it, as Starling exchange moves water across the capillary wall. Guyton's seconds to minutes are short beside a day, and ticket 26's budget says whether that time constant costs steps.
- **Antonius's balance, with three terms dropped.** Human coefficients for the linear albumin and phosphate terms are on hand. The store search found the constants of Kishen's simplified form only for horses. Carbonate's charge is `2 Kd / [H+]` times bicarbonate's, about 0.3% at pH 7.4 with Antonius's pKd of 10.2. `[H+]` and `[OH-]` are each below 0.001 mmol/L at pH 7.4. Dropping all three removes `Kd` and `Kw` and their temperature terms. That is arithmetic on Antonius's Table 1.
- **Hemoglobin's charge does not read saturation.** No source on hand states hemoglobin's oxygen-linked proton charge as a form (No form on hand). Dash 2016's `K5''` and `K6''` enter only the oxygen and CO2 binding terms of its Eqs 4a to 4d. Research 11's carbamino term reads oxygen and carries part of the Haldane effect.
- **Ammonium answers pH with a lag of days.** Rose and Post put the peak at 5 to 6 days, and one memory with time constant `τ` gives that lag. The rise within 2 h, which Rose and Post put down mostly to a more acid urine, is left out. The sensor reads pH, which is dimensionless and so can carry a set point if ticket 18 wants one. No concentration can (research 11).

### What the contract lacks

Research 11's list stands. These are new.

- **A sum across species.** `add` takes one dimension, and the identity table maps a species' concentration only to a ratio with itself. So no kernel can add sodium, potassium and chloride into a strong ion difference. The balance needs a charge concentration, with an identity from each charged species' concentration. Body 01 lists the osmotic identity that osmolality needs for ticket 03. The charge one is new.
- **Mass-action rows.** `K1 [CO2] / [H+]` and the weak-acid terms are concentrations of different ions, and each needs an identity into that charge concentration.
- **A per-space implicit row.** A site field is `row - value` (`defining`, `crates/ng-contract/src/law.rs`), so a value that reads its own row makes the row the balance's residual. Whether compile accepts a field that reads its own row at its own site was not checked.
