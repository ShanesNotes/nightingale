# 68: The ledger's sum of a combination's rows reads one last place off when the rows move among themselves

Spec: [solver contract](../../../docs/architecture/solver-contract.md) §5 (compensated values survive into the certificate), its tradeoffs line on two f64 per stored amount and §7's policy values, `crates/ng-record/src/history.rs` (`LedgerChunk::after`, `History::opening`), `crates/ng-validate/src/certificate.rs` (the closure check), `crates/ng-validate/src/slice.rs` (`arena_certificate`) and `tools/bench/src/link_log.rs`

Type: task

Status: resolved

Claimed by: nc68-build

Blocked by: none

The milestone protocol's 48 h run on `ng_solver::Core::new` at 10 ms refused at [native-contract 52](52-energy-fails-the-sliding-windows.md)'s head: albumin's closure on the window from 06:55:00.002930551 was 2^-96 (1.262177448353619e-29), where the slice allowed it exactly zero, because no route carries albumin. `certify` keeps only the earliest failing window, so the run said nothing of how many failed or of energy.

**The cause.** Albumin is 20 rows that 31 columns inside the body move among themselves: circulation, valves, Starling flow, lymph and the albumin exchange. Each transfer is exact in both rows it moves: over the first two hours of the arena's plan, 1,488,980 steps, the exact sum of albumin's rows never changed. The ledger did not read that exact sum. It summed the rows in compensated arithmetic, one row at a time in row order, and a compensated pair holds about 106 bits. From 08:03:38 a small row holds a bit finer than the total's last place, and the sum must round that bit. Which way it rounds depends on how the rows split the total, not on the total, so the sum moved by one last place, and back, in 43,672 of those steps. A window whose two ends read the sum on different sides of that rounding fails closure. Rows (1000, 3.8173233833909553e-17), (599.9999999999999, -2.158212844549645e-14) and (85, 2.096380695060251e-20), with 7.506156663584488e-24 moved from the second to the third, show it in three rows: each row holds the transfer exactly and the sum moves by -2^-96. The evaluation of a window's end inside a step is not the cause: a curve whose two ends are equal reads that value at every instant.

The short protocols pass because they end before the first fine bit. The scored pick passed because its one window reads the sum at two instants and both fell on the same side.

**A second rounding.** A row cannot hold a transfer finer than its own last place, and two rows of different size round one transfer to different places, so the stored total itself moves. Two reservoirs at 600 and 424 relaxing toward 512, with no route, refuse closure this way. The slice has not shown it yet: over four hours the exact sum never moved, but the rows' least room above their last place fell from 15 bits to 4 at 08:03 and to 2 at 10:41, so a 48 h run may reach it.

**What to build:** the sum a combination holds reads its rows' exact total, rounded once to a compensated pair, so it depends on the total alone; and the closure admits the rounding a row's own update cannot avoid, counted from the window's accepted steps.

- [x] `crates/ng-record/src/history.rs` sums each combination's rows exactly and rounds once to the nearest pair, for each step's chunk and for the opening.
- [x] Tests that fail before the change: the three rows above read one sum before and after the transfer; rows of both signs cancel; a tie in either part goes to even; parts at the smallest and largest exponents; the fold's own result wherever the fold is exact.
- [x] The count of last places one row update may round, derived from the code and held by a test over many generated updates.
- [x] `crates/ng-validate/src/certificate.rs` admits that count for each row update a window's steps make, counted from the body's columns and the two walkers' positions, in the policy's digest and in each result. The two reservoirs refuse at a count of zero and pass at it. A closure at its allowance passes and one last place over refuses.
- [x] `tools/bench/src/link_log.rs` keeps each walker's link before the one it gave, so a walk reads each link once.
- [x] Mutant rows: the total folded again, a tie broken away from even, the low part dropped, the allowance dropped, the steps counted as one.
- [x] The pins that move are updated with their reason.
- [x] The contract's sentences and the glossary's policy value name the exact sum and the count.
- [x] The slice's 4,200 s coverage with a 4,100 s window passes through `certify`, albumin's worst closure reads 0, and the report agrees with the certificate.

## Answer

**The exact total.** `exact_total` in `history.rs` adds every part of every row times its weight into 64-bit limbs from 2^-1074 up, each held in an i128, and reads the sum once: the float nearest it, ties to even, then the float nearest what that leaves, joined by `plus` so the pair stays normalized. `LedgerChunk::after` and `History::opening` both read it. A zero part adds nothing and is skipped, and a lone row of unit weight, as each of the body's held pools is, is read as the limbs would read it without them, so the sum costs 0.67% of the slice's instructions and 0.50% of the body's. Every combination's weights are whole numbers, since the basis holds them as `i64`: water, each species and energy weigh each row 1, and charge weighs each ion by its valence, -1, 1 or 2 on the whole body. A part's 53 bits times a weight's 64 fit an i128, so every kind sums exactly. A row or part that is not finite gives the fold's total, which carries it and is not finite. A sum of finite parts past the finite range gives a pair whose larger part is infinite, read from the limbs and not from the fold: the fold reads such a sum as finite when `scaled` rounds a weight past 2^53, and when small rows round away against its low part at unit weights. Either way the comparison fails closed. The only other place that sums rows into a combination's total is admission's held-total check (`total` in `crates/ng-contract/src/admission.rs`). It judges the start state against a tolerance and rounds to one float, so an exact sum changes nothing there, and it lies under the contract crate. Nothing in `tools/bench/` sums rows.

**The count for one row update.** A row update is `amount.plus(net.scaled(coefficient))` (`next_state` in `crates/ng-contract/src/quadrature.rs`). Write u = 2^-53 and a total's last place as 2^(e - 105), with 2^e the power of two at or below the total's larger part, so a total of (4096, -6.39e-14) takes 2^-93, and u^2 · 2^(e + 1) is one last place. `plus` is the accurate double-word sum of Joldes, Muller and Popescu (ACM Transactions on Mathematical Software 44(2), 2017, its Algorithm 6, with `two_sum` in place of its fast two-sums), whose result is within 3u^2 + 13u^3 of the exact sum, relative to it. At a unit coefficient `scaled` is exact: `two_product` by ±1 has no error, and the low part times ±1 is exact. So the update rounds under (3 + 13u) u^2 of the new row, under 4 last places of any total at least that large. At any other coefficient `scaled` rounds twice, the low part's product and its sum with the high product's error, each under u of a part under 2u of the product: under 3u^2 of the transfer. With the row and the transfer no larger than the total, the two together round under 6 + O(u) last places, so under 7. The policy value, `ROW_ROUNDING` in `slice.rs`, is 7, which holds for either kind. Every column term of the slice and of the whole body that touches a combination has a unit coefficient today, so 4 would do now. `a_row_update_rounds_under_4_last_places_at_a_unit_coefficient_and_under_7_at_any_other` holds both counts against the exact total over 200,000 generated updates; the most it found was 0.75 of a last place at either kind.

**The allowance.** `CertificatePolicy::admitting_row_rounding` counts each combination's row updates a step from the compiled body's columns: each column term whose row is in the combination (`arena_certificate` sets it). `certify` counts the steps a window meets from its two walkers: each walker counts the segments it has taken, one an accepted step, so a stretch's windows meet at most the trailing segment, the leading one and every one between. A window's closure may then also be 7 times the updates a step times those steps, in last places of the combination's total at the window's late end. `admitted` scales the place by the count as a float: exact up to 2^53, to within one part in 2^53 above it, and held at the largest signed integer past that. Either end serves a combination no route moves, whose total is the same at every step to within the allowance itself. For one a route moves, every step's total is at most the late end's plus the window's gross exchange, and the rounding on that part is under 7 · updates · steps · 2u^2 of the gross, under 1e-21 of it on the slice over a day, far inside the relative floor's 1e-12. The two readings of the total round under half a last place each, inside the slack between 6 and 7. A window's end inside a step also reads a chord through `between`, one more `plus`: a model of it rounded at most 0.125 of a last place, which the room between 3 and 7 covers once a window meets two updates. Each result reports the policy value (`row_rounding`), the last places its worst window admitted and that window's last place, and the policy's digest holds the count and every combination's updates a step.

**Where the bound holds.** It holds for a combination whose weighted rows share one sign, so no row exceeds the total. On both the slice and the whole body, the only combination of both signs is charge (69 rows on the slice, 230 on the body), and a route moves it on both. Its total, about 1e-9 on the slice, sits far below its rows, so its last place bounds nothing there; its relative floor on gross exchange holds it, as before. Every other combination's weights are all 1 on amounts that cannot be negative.

**What it used.** The two reservoirs at 600 and 424, 6 h at 100 ms with a 3 h window, refuse closure with no rounding admitted and pass at 7: the worst window used 364 last places of the 2,268,021 it admitted, and the test pins that count (`tests/closure_rounding.rs`). Two reservoirs that start at 0.5 each and take 2^20 from 2 h 50 min to 6 h pass with no floor at the late end's place, which the result reports for its worst window, and the early end's place refuses them. On the slice, the 4,200 s coverage with a 4,100 s window from 06:55 on `Core::new` at 10 ms (911,014 steps, 39,503 window starts) passes through `certify`. Albumin's worst closure reads 0, where the base refused it at 2^-96 from the window at 18.677659 s. Of the combinations a route moves, the most any window used was energy's 6 last places of the 224,535,780 its window admitted, then potassium's 4.4, sodium's 3.1, chloride's 1.9, vasopressin's 1.5 and water's 0.77. Every combination no route moves reads 0. Charge passes on its relative floor, as before. The report walks every window again and agrees with the certificate bit for bit.

**The bench's link log.** A walk newest first asks next for the link it read as the one before. Each of `LinkLog`'s two cursors now keeps that link's bytes and hands them back, so a walk reads each link once, always, with no switch. `two_walks_hand_back_each_link_as_kept_reading_each_link_once` walks a two-reservoir history with two interleaved walkers: every link expands to the bytes it was kept as, and the two walks read one link for each link they give, where reading each with the one before reads two less one.

**The pins.** The opening and every chunk read the exact total, so each seal over the ledger moves once: `trace.rs`'s three first-second seals of the slice, the generative driver's `SMOKE_SEAL`, which a test holds equal to the `Core::new` one, and `body_run.rs`'s `ADMITTED_SEAL` and the body's first-second seal, which chains from it. The slice's policy digest in `tests/arena_certificate.rs` moves once, because the digest now holds the count and each combination's row updates a step. No model hash, packet hash, trace line or run digest moves.

**The scored pick.** Its record in [foundation 01](../../foundation/issues/01-arena-and-milestone.md) stands as recorded: the run's seal was made with the old sum, so a rerun at this head would print another seal and smaller closures, and its run digest, its model hash `42c60687` and its verdict stand.

**Left, not built.**
- The early walker reads the second day to reach the first. Starting it at the first day's link needs that link's seal, which only a walk or the store's own index gives, so it is a change to `ConservationView` and `certify`.
- A refusal reports the first failing quantity and window, not every quantity.
- Transfers made exact under the contract crate, which only a fixed-point ledger gives.
- A scale for a combination of both signs, such as charge, in place of its total's last place.
- The cumulative exchange's rounding on a route that has carried much and then carries almost nothing, but not nothing, for a whole window. Neither term of the allowance covers a gross flow under steps · columns · 3.7e-20 of the cumulative flow, so a correct run would be refused there. No run shows it, and the milestone protocol's routes are ten orders of magnitude away.
- `scaled` takes its integer as a float, exact to 2^53. An integer is scaled at a column's coefficient in `next_state` (1 at most today), at the exchange weight in `LedgerChunk::after` (2 at most), at `admitted`'s count, in the fold of a part that is not finite, and at nanosecond counts under 2^53. Only `admitted` can meet one past 2^53, on a window of more than 2^53 / (7 · updates) steps.
- The generative driver's `capture.rs:612` still folds each combination's opening inventory, so the capture's first edge can sit one last place from the ledger's opening. The generative line is parked.
- The count is read from the body's columns, which the basis digest does not seal, so a body of the same basis with other columns gives another count. The policy's digest records the count used.
